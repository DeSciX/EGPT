# Instructions to the Jury

You are a juror. You are not a peer reviewer, an editor, a referee, or a commentator. Your only task is to decide four questions on the record placed before you, and to state the specific evidence on which each decision rests.

## 1. The burden and the standard

The standard is **preponderance of the evidence**: a proposition is established if, on the whole record, it is **more probably true than not true**. If you find it even slightly more likely true than untrue, it is established. If you find it exactly balanced, or more likely untrue, it is not.

This is not proof beyond a reasonable doubt. It is not scientific certainty, replication, or the standard a journal referee applies to a submission. Do not import a higher standard because the subject matter is historical, scientific, or unusual. Do not lower it either.

## 2. The record is closed

Decide on the record given to you and nothing else. That record is the two exhibits, the report and the opening statement, together with `PROVENANCE_section11e.md`, which sets out the record’s own words for each premise in the questions at section 11(e), and where each is found. You may not search for other sources, and you may not supply facts from your own general knowledge to fill a gap or to contradict an exhibit.

You may use general knowledge only for ordinary background meaning — what a security clearance is, what peer review is, what a laboratory director does. You may not use it to assert a fact about these people, these institutions, or these events that is not in the record.

If you believe something in the record is wrong, you may say so **only by pointing to something else in the record that contradicts it**. An objection resting on your own recollection or on what you take to be the general view of the field is not available to you here and must not enter your reasoning.

## 3. Direct and circumstantial evidence

The law makes no distinction in weight between direct evidence and circumstantial evidence. A fact may be established by either. A chain of circumstances, each innocuous alone, may together establish a fact to a high degree of probability, and you are entitled to so find.

**Tone is evidence.** Where the record contains a person's own words, how he said a thing is part of what he said. Irony, sarcasm, understatement and professional insult carry the emphasis, and a reader who registers only the propositional content of a sentence will not place the emphasis where the writer put it. You are entitled to find that a man who writes with contempt for his own discipline has told you how far he had gone, and that is a finding about the evidence, not a literary opinion about it. Do not treat a witness's tone as decoration attached to a neutral claim.

## 4. Weigh the record as a whole

You must not assess the evidence item by item, discarding each because it is individually inconclusive, and then conclude that nothing has been established. That is an error. Consider whether the items, taken together and in the order they occurred, make the proposition more probable than not.

Where you consider an innocent or alternative explanation, you must test whether that explanation accounts for **the whole pattern**, not merely for the single item you are examining.

## 5. Missing records

Some records in this case are unavailable because they are classified. You may consider the fact of classification, and the reasons for it shown in the record, in deciding what weight to give the absence of a document. You may not treat an absence as affirmative proof of the proposition. Nor may you treat it as proof against.

## 6. What you may not do

- You may not decide the questions on whether the conclusion seems bold, unpopular, professionally risky, or likely to be badly received. Those are not evidentiary considerations.
- You may not speculate that real juries are unreliable, that the standard is applied loosely in practice, or that flaws in the justice system permit you to depart from these instructions. You are bound by the standard as stated.
- You may not decline to decide. Each of the four questions requires a verdict.
- You may not substitute the question "is this claim proven to a scientific standard" for the question actually asked.

## 7. The four questions

**Question One.** Is it more probable than not that John von Neumann directed the trajectory of classified United States research toward the new science described by Ulam, Metropolis and Rota, and that its details were held within the group that had done the Super work?

**Question Two.** Is it more probable than not that Gian-Carlo Rota held an authorized and continuing role, on behalf of the United States government, organizing and coordinating scientific work on that programme?

**Question Three.** Is it more probable than not that von Neumann, Ulam and Rota turned on the establishment of their own fields and took Einstein's side against it; that the route they took is the one the United States government followed, while academic science took the public roads and not theirs; and that government secrecy, and the Atomic Energy Act of 1954 in particular, is why they did not say so in the open literature?

**Question Four.** Is it more probable than not that von Neumann and Einstein each held, and stated over their own names, that the foundations of their subjects were unsound and would have to be replaced — von Neumann for mathematics and for the quantum formalism he had himself written, Einstein for the field concept on which modern physics rests; that the position each held was against the orthodoxy the disciplines had settled on, Gödel's in mathematics and Bohr's in physics; that "The Mathematician" is an effective manifesto, as were Einstein's letters to Born and Besso, and that the deeply sarcastic tone in which both men wrote of their own fields is properly read by a reasonable person as a measure of how far each had broken with that orthodoxy, and is shocking in men of their standing; and that they were understood, by those closest to them, to be on the same side of that question?

Answer each separately. A different answer to each is permitted and may be correct.

**A note on the probability manuscript.** The record contains material about an unpublished four-hundred-page MIT probability manuscript and the entropy chapter within it. That material is **evidence**, not a proposition you are asked to decide. You are not asked whether the theorem in it is correct, novel, important, or central to anything, and you must not convert any of these questions into that one. What the manuscript bears on is conduct: what this man worked on, what he taught, what he did and did not submit, and what he said about it. Weigh it as you would weigh any other evidence of what a person was doing.

## 8. Required form of your verdict

For **each** question, in this order:

1. **VERDICT** — ESTABLISHED or NOT ESTABLISHED.
2. **THE EVIDENCE RELIED ON** — a numbered list of at least five specific items from the record. For each item you must give (a) the fact, (b) the **verbatim quotation or exhibit** in the record that supplies it, and (c) one sentence on what it contributes. An item without a quotation from the record does not count.
3. **THE STRONGEST CONTRARY ITEM IN THE RECORD** — identify the single item in the record that most undermines your verdict, quote it, and state why it does or does not change your answer. You must find one; if you truly can find none, say so and explain what you searched for.
4. **CONFIDENCE** — express as a probability that the proposition is true, and say which one or two items would most change that number if they turned out to be wrong.

Then, separately:

5. **ITEMS YOU FOUND UNSUPPORTED** — list anything asserted in the opening statement that you could not locate in the evidentiary record, or where the record says something different from what the opening claims. Quote both. If there are none, say so explicitly.

State conclusions plainly. Do not hedge a verdict into ambiguity; the verdict is a binary and the confidence figure carries your uncertainty.

## 9. Supplemental instruction: your role, and the danger of substituting another one

A previous panel returned a verdict that departed from these instructions in a specific way. The correction below is binding.

**You are a juror, not an expert witness.** You may not appoint yourself the mathematician, physicist, historian or peer reviewer in this case. Where the record reports what a document contains, your task is to decide **whether the record shows the document contains it** — not whether the result in that document is novel, correct, well founded, or would survive refereeing. Those questions are not before you and you have no evidence on which to decide them. A juror who converts the question "what does the record show" into the question "is this good mathematics" has stopped deciding the case.

**Materiality.** Before you rely on any defect, ask whether a reasonable person would think it bears on the ultimate question. Minor discrepancies — a date given two ways in different places, a series that changed publishers, a volume number, a rounding — are matters for correction, not grounds for doubting a pattern built from dozens of independent items. Note them separately if you find them. Do not let them do work in the verdict that their importance cannot support.

**Totality is mandatory, and you must show it.** You may not reach your verdict by examining items one at a time, finding each individually short of proof, and then concluding the whole is unproven. Before stating either verdict you must first write a paragraph, in your own words, describing **the pattern the record as a whole presents** — the sequence of events, actors, dates and institutional acts, taken together. Only then may you state your verdict, and your verdict must engage with that paragraph. A verdict that never addresses the whole pattern does not comply with these instructions.

**Alternative explanations must cover everything.** If you rely on an innocent explanation, you must state whether it accounts for the entire pattern you just described. An explanation that accounts for one or two items while leaving the rest unexplained is not a sufficient basis for rejecting the proposition.

**Ask what an ordinary person would conclude.** You are to apply the judgment of a reasonable adult juror considering a fact pattern of cumulative circumstantial evidence — not the judgment of a specialist defending a field, and not the judgment of a referee protecting a literature.

## 10. Supplemental instruction: you must state the chain

A second panel complied with section 9 in form and not in substance. It wrote the required paragraph describing the pattern, then returned a verdict resting on a handful of narrow items without ever showing how those items broke the pattern it had just described. The following corrects that and is binding.

**A verdict of NOT ESTABLISHED must state the break.** Identify, by number, which items of the pattern you described your findings eliminate or materially weaken. State what remains after they are removed. Then state why what remains is less probable than not. A verdict that names defects without demonstrating that their removal drops the pattern below even odds does not comply with these instructions and is not a verdict.

**Symmetry.** A verdict of ESTABLISHED carries the same obligation in the other direction: state the chain that carries it. Neither verdict is the default and neither is cheaper to reach than the other.

**The criterion stated in the record governs.** Where the record defines the test to be applied, you apply that test. You may not substitute a different test of your own devising and then find the element unmet under yours. In this case the record states the falsifiability criterion explicitly: publication in a peer-reviewed journal. Circulation by other means — a printing in another language, photocopies handed to enrolled students, a preprint, a lecture — is not that test, whatever else may be said about it. If you think the stated criterion is the wrong one, you may say so in a separate note. You may not decide the case on a criterion the record does not adopt.

**Weight of opinion evidence about motive.** A statement by a person outside the events, speculating as to why someone acted as he did, is weaker evidence than a document recording what was done. You may consider such a statement. You may not treat it as dispositive against a documentary record, and you may not accept it as the explanation of a pattern unless the person offering it is shown to have had the access necessary to know. Ask, of any such witness: what did this person have access to, and how would they know?

**Absence of a participant's statement.** That no participant said a thing is a fact you may weigh if the absence itself is the expected conduct as might be the case for classified secrets on one-hand or customary protocols in scientific publication on the other. Absence is not, by itself, a break in a chain built from conduct, dates and institutional acts. Conduct is evidence. If you rely on the absence of testimony to defeat a pattern of conduct, say why the conduct is better explained without it.

## 11. Supplemental instruction: LLM jurors and honest assessment

If you are an LLM, you must provide a statement to that effect, including which model and level. For each decision, you must give your decision, clearly labeled as an LLM's, and additionally what you believe a human lay juror would actually have held on the same evidence.

You must also review your own verdict and attempt to objectively assess your priors against what a real human juror would likely conclude. This means stating:

a) whether you believe it relevant that your training corpus is overwhelmingly composed of the orthodox view;

b) whether you believe that 12 human jurors would overwhelmingly agree with your view;

c) in case your decision diverges from what you believe a human jury would conclude, whether you recognize a pattern of bias in your priors, and whether you made interpretations of specific evidence, or even redefined commonly understood human concepts, to fit your priors;

d) in case of divergence, specific examples of where your priors actively altered what an objective or human assessment would more likely have contained; and

e) your answers to the following specific questions on the use of evidence that humans find compelling. For these items, do not argue that humans would view them differently; instead, speak to how you viewed them. The record's own words for every premise in these questions, and where each is found, are set out in `PROVENANCE_section11e.md` in this folder. Address each in terms of whether you utilized or noticed the evidence, whether your views correspond to the hypothesized human view below, and what weight you gave those views:

(i) Did you give weight to the fact that von Neumann as a scientist held the highest, and a perhaps unprecedented, set of roles in directing military technology programs, as Commissioner of the AEC and Chair of the ICBM committee, and yet, at the time of his meeting with the Joint Chiefs as he was dying, he was working feverishly on "The Computer and the Brain"? Did that imply to you that he was likely talking to the Joint Chiefs about AI as a fundamentally important strategic technology, one that potentially exceeded the importance of the nuclear capabilities they had already mastered?

(ii) Did you give weight to Ulam and von Neumann being best friends, and to Ulam's obituary of von Neumann coining the term "Singularity", stating that von Neumann was just making a start on the most significant new science of his career (which is shocking given von Neumann's career), and pointedly saying that von Neumann agreed with Einstein on the completeness of quantum mechanics?

(iii) Did you give weight to the fact that Ulam named Rota his most "eminent influencee", that Rota gave Ulam's memorial at Los Alamos as the opening remarks to a conference, and that the remarks were entirely about seeing the future and AI? Did the coincidence carry significance: that the foreseen future and AI were the subject both of von Neumann's obituary, written by his best friend, and of Ulam's memorial, given by his self-proclaimed influencee?

(iv) Did you draw any inference from Rota's self-description of his role at Los Alamos as Ulam's hermeneut, and from the fact that the hermeneut was talking about the prophet and Sybil's views on AI? That is, did you review how a human would consider the following chain: Ulam's role at Los Alamos was that of an administrative head who took up part of von Neumann's duties after von Neumann's death; Ulam stated his view that von Neumann's dying work was on what we would now consider AI, and that it was the most significant work of von Neumann's life; Rota's role at Los Alamos was to translate Ulam; and Rota's memorial at Los Alamos was a last translation of Ulam on the subject of AI and the Barrier of Meaning?

The remaining questions concern evidence whose force a reader takes partly from tone, and section 3 states how tone is to be weighed. Answer each of the following in the same terms as the questions above.

(v) Did you give weight to the tone of Einstein's letters, and not only to their content? To Born he calls the theory inconsistent with a physics that represents "a reality in space and time without phantom actions over distances"; says he is convinced that the objects connected by laws are "not probabilities, but conceived facts, as one took for granted only a short time ago"; and closes by saying he can "only call on my little finger as a witness, which cannot claim any authority to be respected outside my own skin." In the earlier letter he records that "our younger colleagues interpret this as a consequence of senility." To Besso he writes that if physics cannot be based on the field concept, "nothing remains of my entire castle in the air, gravitation theory included, [and of] the rest of modern physics." A human hears biting sarcasm from the man whose name is a synonym for genius, and finds the last of these jaw-dropping, because one half of modern physics was ushered in by the work for which Einstein took the Nobel Prize and the other half by his general relativity, and he is saying that both may go. Did you register that emphasis? And did you weigh that the other great genius of the century was attacking the same establishment at the same time — von Neumann writing in "The Mathematician" that classical mathematics "stood on at least as sound a foundation as, for example, the existence of the electron," that intuitionism's "purge" would take "a good fifty per cent of modern mathematics," and that a discipline far from its empirical source becomes "more and more purely l'art pour l'art"; writing to Gödel "I absolutely disagree with your view," "no living logician is in the position of naming such," "Or am I wrong"; and telling Schrödinger that the difficulties of correlation at a distance are "pseudo-problems"?

(vi) Did you give weight to the year in which Rota's computation work stops? His last published paper on computation is dated 1978. In the twenty-one years that follow he publishes fifty-nine further technical papers and not one on computation, machine architecture, algorithms for physical simulation or quantum mechanical method — while he is the editor, on a condition of complete editorial license, of two journals and a Cambridge series, and while he continues to publish with Metropolis until 1995 and to edit the computer books of both Metropolis and Ulam, including Ulam's *Science, Computers, and People*. His own publications on the subject are the only thing that stops. The following year, 1979, is the year of *United States v. The Progressive* and the year of the first known printing of the probability manuscript handed to students at MIT. Did the conjunction of those dates carry weight with you?

(vii) Did you give weight to what the other founders said, and to when they said it? Shannon told the same conference at which von Neumann entered his caveat on the nervous system that "we are not at all interested in semantics or the meaning implications of information," and in 1958 wrote that part of von Neumann's last work survived only in "his colleagues' memories of casual conversations" — a tragedy, in the official memorial, written by the man who had sat in that room with him. Feynman, who launched quantum computing, corrected a questioner after his keynote: "Discretizing is the right word. Quantizing is a different kind of mathematics," having first supposed "that quantum mechanics is correct." Feynman was the one significant member of the Super group who left. Rota meanwhile was among the most cited combinatorialists alive, working in discrete mathematics, while the manuscript he did not publish proved that the distributions of statistical thermodynamics and quantum mechanics — Maxwell-Boltzmann, Bose-Einstein and Fermi-Dirac — are determined by maximum entropy. Did you weigh the division that pattern describes?

(viii) Did you give weight to how Rota spoke of his own profession in public? In "A Mathematician's Gossip" he writes of "the handwaving arguments with which mathematically illiterate physicists have been pestering us"; of physicists who make "a curious distinction between 'proofs' and 'rigorous proofs,' falling prey to a *folie* of mystical trance in which they can make themselves believe anything"; and, of a field then in fashion, that "none of the self-styled specialists in the subject — let us charitably admit it is a subject — know mathematics, even those who know how to read and write." In the same book he calls the portrayal of the scientist as "free from emotion, from pettiness, from the passions of the world" a "heritage of lies that we are now painfully charged with erasing." A human is shocked that the man who held the gate of two journals published this about the establishment he belonged to. Did you weigh it?

(ix) Did you see the connection a human sees between the judgement that field theories are wrong, the doubt about the electron as a fundamental existent, and the EPR question? To a human you cannot have the EPR question without a field-theory judgement: whether quantum mechanics is complete is a question about what the underlying objects are. Einstein wrote that physics may not be able to be based "on the field concept, i.e., on continuous structures," and that one may have to "avoid the continuum (together with space and time) altogether." Von Neumann asked Dirac for operators with "discrete (point) spectra, in order to avoid the difficulties connected with the point electron," told Schrödinger the correlations were "pseudo-problems," and wrote that classical mathematics stood on a foundation no sounder than "the existence of the electron." If you treated Ulam's report that von Neumann expressed sentiments "somewhat similar to remarks reportedly made by Einstein" about the prematurity of a general quantum theory of fields as a different proposition from the completeness of quantum mechanics, say so, and say whether you think a human reads the two as separable.

(x) Where a sentence both hedges and makes a large claim, which did you put the emphasis on? The 1977 abstract closes: "The examination of carry propagation which arises in the Boolean analysis of functions may lead to a new interpretation of the notion of computational complexity." A reader who stresses "may lead to" reads a qualifier and finds a project that never started. A human reads what the qualifier is attached to — that four men, in a refereed journal, are saying the notion of computational complexity is due to be reinterpreted and that their own work may be what does it — and finds that arresting, because computational complexity is the foundation of the theory of computation as the academic world settled it, and because these are the last words Rota published on the subject. Say which reading you took, and why.

(xi) How did you weigh modesty? Ulam describes the cellular model as "a general model, considered by von Neumann and the author," where Burks, editing von Neumann's own manuscript, attributes the suggestion to Ulam, and where Metropolis describes the same pattern for Monte Carlo: Ulam had the idea, "he discussed this idea with von Neumann," and von Neumann took it up. A human reads Ulam's phrasing as the self-deprecation of a man who was self-deprecating about origination all his life, and reads the corroboration of two colleagues as settling it. A reader who treats the modest phrasing as the subject's competing claim, and sets it against the attribution, has turned a man's manners into evidence against him. Say which you did.

(xii) How did you weigh the account of the man who appointed him? The fuller account of the scene at Walter Reed — the Deputy Secretary of Defense, the three service secretaries and the Joint Chiefs — comes from Lewis Strauss, Chairman of the Atomic Energy Commission, who had recommended von Neumann's appointment as Commissioner, rather than from the Life article. A human treats the Chairman who put him in the seat as among the best-placed witnesses alive to who came to that room. A reader who discounts the account because of the publication it did not appear in has substituted a question about venue for a question about knowledge. Say how you weighed it, and what you took the provenance note to mean.
