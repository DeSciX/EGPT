# Statement of the author on the debate record

*Essam Abadir, on what the 18.313x P=NP debate was and what it was not.*

The debate logs were not initially meant as a recorded proof of the process. The debate was an
exploratory system of agentic peer-review, developed on the fly, to put the proofs through the
strictest adversarial scrutiny I could arrange — given that traditional peer review was closed to
me, as the record of the Lean forums after April 2025 shows.

It is not expected to be a perfect system, and I do not claim that it is one. It was built while
it was being used, and it changed as it went.

The debate logs and their history are all recorded in git and are offered here for transparency,
not as a certificate. **The proofs are public.** Anyone able to find the flaw in them is
encouraged to do so, with or without the aid of these logs.

## On the exchanges that were reconstructed

Some exchange files were not captured directly from a session transcript. Where a transcript
failed — context compaction, a crashed session — the exchange was reconstructed **in the moment,
from the agent memories as they stood at the time**, not afterwards from recollection or inference.
The reconstruction was part of running the debate, not a later repair of the record.

What is published is what was actually recorded at the time, as I had it during the process. The
system was not perfect, and I do not present it as one.

## On the exchanges 22-33 concordance

Those exchange files were assembled during the session from the accumulated state file and the
outcome ledger, at a point when the full session transcript had not yet been exported. The verbatim
agent outputs the moderator still held at the time were written out separately and are in
`RAW_AGENT_OUTPUTS_22_33.md`; the complete session transcript was exported afterwards and is in
`session_transcript/`. A reader who wants to check an exchange against what was actually said can
do so from those two files.

A note previously sat in this folder speculating about what the assembled files "may have"
done — smoothed over contentious moments, omitted objections, compressed responses — and ranking
the files as primary, secondary and tertiary sources. It was written by the same system whose
habits this record documents, it asserted a characterisation of an agent's reaction for which it
gave no source, and its stated action — export the session transcript — had since been carried
out. It was speculation about a record, presented as a finding about it. I have removed it, and
this statement stands in its place.

I make no claim about what the assembled files smoothed or omitted, in either direction. The
primary material is published beside them. Anyone assessing this record, in particular anyone
arguing against me from it, should compare the files themselves and reach their own conclusion.

## On evidentiary grades

Earlier versions of this record carried grades classifying each exchange by how it had been
captured. Those grades were produced by an adversarial system — the same one whose reflexes the
record documents — and I did not review each of them. They are not mine and they are removed.

The evidence is the evidence. Each exchange file still carries the `sourceFormat` field it was
written with; that field is part of the primary data and has not been altered, but no weight is
claimed for it here and the author adopts no classification built on it. Anyone assessing this
record — in particular anyone arguing against me from it — should do their own review, and is
expected to.
