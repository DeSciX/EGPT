Returning to Los Alamos in 1957 after a year’s leave of
absence at MIT, I was asked by Bradbury to accept one of
the two newly created positions of research advisor to the
director of the laboratory. The other advisor was to be John
Manley, a physicist who had held important administrative
posts at Los Alamos during the war and wanted to return to
New Mexico after a long absence as professor at the Univer-
sity of Washington in Seattle. Administratively, research ad-
visors were to be on the same level as division leaders, and
their duties were to oversee the research activities through-
