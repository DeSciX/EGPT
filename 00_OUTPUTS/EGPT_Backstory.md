### von Neumann's Lost Theorem \- everything is computable, a.k.a. P \= NP

> "Incidentally, the other day I developed a method that always allows a finite decision for the effective provability question concerning propositions that are built up solely by means of the concepts 'not', 'or' (thus also 'and', 'follows', etc.), \[and\] 'provable' (starting from the identical truth-consistency is for example such a proposition). >   
> … I absolutely disagree with your view on the formalizability of intuitionism. …  
>   
> … is it not a fact, that not a single construction of the kind mentioned is known that cannot be formalized in A, and that no living logician is in the position of naming such \[a construction\]? Or am I wrong\[?\]"  
> *John von Neumann, letter to Kurt Gödel, Berlin, 12 January 1931\. Kurt Gödel,* Collected Works, Vol. V.

**My lay translation.** Paragraph one's "'not', 'or' (thus also 'and', 'follows', etc.)" is what mathematicians call conjunctive normal form (CNF), and it is how they express NP-hard problems like circuit satisfiability and the travelling salesman problem. He's opening the letter by saying he has a jaw dropping proof, then casually challenging Gödel to see whether he can figure it out, or whether Gödel is going to have to ask to see the proof \- a major flex. Paragraphs two and three are incredibly intense language for the universally loved von Neumann talking to his greatest professional rival. He's saying Gödel's incompleteness theorems don't touch von Neumann's newly adopted system, called Intuitionism. "Or am I wrong?"

This 1931 letter needs a little context. It is a few months after Kurt Gödel announced his first incompleteness theorem at a 1930 conference in Königsberg**.** The lead-up to the letter is a crisis in mathematics where initially von Neumann is representing the famed David Hilbert, known as “the Sage” of mathematics. At Königsberg, von Neumann is crushed by Gödel and Hilbert’s Program is effectively dead. So, a few months later, von Neumann is on the rebound and in this 1931 letter von Neumann is the figurative Babe Ruth, leaving the Red Sox, to join the Yankees, in order to beat the Giants.

This **crisis in the foundations of mathematics** of the early twentieth century arose when paradoxes in set theory and questions about infinity, consistency, and mathematical truth undermined confidence that classical mathematics rested on secure foundations. Three principal camps emerged. **Logicism**, associated especially with Frege and Russell, sought to show that mathematics could ultimately be derived from logic. **Formalism**, championed by Hilbert and represented by von Neumann, proposed treating mathematics as rigorously specified symbolic systems and proving by secure, preferably finitistic means that those systems were consistent. **Intuitionism**, founded by Brouwer and developed by Heyting, rejected the idea that mathematical truth exists independently of construction: a mathematical object or proposition is legitimate only insofar as it can be constructed or demonstrated, leading intuitionists to reject unrestricted use of the law of excluded middle and significant portions of classical mathematics involving completed infinity. The dispute became known as the **Grundlagenstreit**—the foundational struggle—with Hilbert and Brouwer its most prominent antagonists.

* **1926–27:** von Neumann went to **Göttingen on a Rockefeller fellowship specifically to work as Hilbert's assistant**. He arrived in the fall of 1926 and worked in the Hilbert circle during the **1926–27 academic year**. At the same time Hilbert was lecturing on the mathematical foundations of quantum mechanics; von Neumann attended, and this interaction contributed to their joint work with Lothar Nordheim on quantum mechanics. * **1928:** Hilbert–Brouwer engaged in a bitter conflict culminating in Brouwer's removal from *Mathematische Annalen.* Einstein’s distaste for how Hilbert did this resulted in Einstein’s refusal to join the new board. This was therefore only about a year after von Neumann's period working directly with Hilbert. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2019/entries/brouwer/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  
* **September 1930, Königsberg:** This conference was explicitly organized around the three competing foundational schools. **Carnap represented logicism, Heyting represented Brouwer’s intuitionism, and von Neumann represented formalism.** So von Neumann was literally the public spokesman for **Hilbert's camp**. [American Mathematical Society](https://www.ams.org/journals/bull/2013-50-03/S0273-0979-2012-01403-9/S0273-0979-2012-01403-9.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com). And, remarkably, **this is the same Königsberg meeting at which Gödel announced incompleteness**.  
    
  In short, von Neumann was reeling in 1930, independently derived Gödel second incompleteness theorem, and in previous letters von Neumann graciously wrote Gödel that he would allow Gödel precedence.

In a few months, relative to Gödel, von Neumann went from the defeated representative of Hilbert’s Logicism to the defiant champion of Hilbert’s bitter rival, Brouwer and his Intuitionism. ## Two Eulogies: the Singularity and the Barrier of Meaning

There is no way to understand what I was handed without the right context, and that context comes from Stanislaw Ulam's memorial and obituary of John von Neumann.

The Singularity is a term coined by Ulam in his 1958 obituary of von Neumann but it is neither a dystopian future nor some idealistic vision \- it is an approaching reality that is different. Ulam leaves it open for us, the future people, to direct whether the change is good or bad:

> "One conversation centered on the ever accelerating progress of technology and changes in the mode of human life, which gives the appearance of approaching some essential singularity in the history of the race beyond which human affairs, as we know them, could not continue."  
*Stanislaw Ulam, "John von Neumann, 1903–1957,"* Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society *64:3, Part 2, p. 5, May 1958\.*

The "Barrier of Meaning" is a term coined by Rota in his 1985 memorial of Ulam. These are Ulam's words, as Rota reports them:

> "Your friends in AI are now beginning to trumpet the role of contexts, but they are not practising their lesson. They still want to build machines that see by imitating cameras, perhaps with some feedback thrown in. Such an approach is bound to fail, since it starts out with a logical misunderstanding."  
>   
**What Does It Mean To Understand Something?**

Richard Feynman’s famous lectures in physics are strangely philosophical. He opens the discussion of the double slit experiment with the following:

What do we mean by “understanding” something? We can imagine that this complicated array of moving things which constitutes “the world” is something like a great chess game being played by the gods, and we are observers of the game. …  
The rules of the game are what we mean by fundamental physics …  
If we know the rules, we consider that we “understand” the world.”  
Richard Feynman · The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Volume I · Chapter 2, §2-1 "Physics before 1920"

>   
> "I now take this object out of my pocket and ask you to look at it. No amount of staring at this object will ever tell you that this is a key, unless you already have some previous familiarity with the way keys are used."  
*Stanislaw Ulam, quoted in Gian-Carlo Rota, "The Barrier of Meaning,"* Letters in Mathematical Physics *10 (1985), pp. 97–99.*

Ulam's jab about cameras is not about optics. A camera produces what von Neumann called a *complete code* \- every pulse specified, every pixel spelled out, nothing left for the receiver to work out. von Neumann's last argument, the one he was still writing when he died, is that the brain does not run on complete codes at all: "whatever language the central nervous system is using, it is characterized by less logical and arithmetical depth than what we are normally used to," and that language "may well correspond to a *short code* … rather than to a complete code: when we talk mathematics, we may be discussing a secondary language, built on the primary language truly used by the central nervous system." 

Ulam, summarizing his friend's unfinished book, put it in his own words: the second kind of communication in the brain "is a kind of general language … which may correspond to the short code," and "must be quite different from languages in the usual sense. It may, for example, operate with analogies." That is the whole point of the cameras remark. Physics and computing were languages to these two men before they were anything else \- von Neumann called logic and mathematics "historical, accidental forms of expression," no more necessary than Greek or Sanskrit \- and a machine fed nothing but the complete code is a machine that never learns the language.

Ulam said we are in desperate need of philosophy to answer this question:

> "Philosophers and logicians, from Duns Scotus in the Twelfth Century to Ludwig Wittgenstein only yesterday, have done a lot of clear thinking about all this stuff. If your friends in AI persist in ignoring their past, they will be condemned to repeat it, at a high cost that will be borne by the taxpayers."  
*Stanislaw Ulam, quoted in Gian-Carlo Rota, "The Barrier of Meaning,"* Letters in Mathematical Physics *10 (1985), pp. 97–99.*

Now the world is finally starting to talk about the relationship between compression and intelligence. Wonderful commentators like [3Blue1Brown](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l6DKRf-fAAM) and [Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KZeIEiBrT_w) are starting to tell the Team Reality story in the AI world. But for us, LLM’s are old news \- they were always going to regurgitate *intelligent sounding* words if you threw enough compute at them, but they were never going to *understand*. 

On 23 March 1950 at the Seventh Macy Conference on Cybernetics, Claude Shannon reviewed LLMs as a proof-of-concept based on his 1948 paper called “The Entropy of the English Language”  \- incidentally von Neumann was on the program with him that day. It gave his measurement of the redundancy of printed English, and opened by saying precisely what he was not doing: "we are not at all interested in semantics or the meaning implications of information … it may not have any meaning at all." What he produced was a number. English is roughly 50% redundant; the shortest code exists, and you can compute how short. That is the ceiling on entropy encoding, and it is a ceiling on compression, not on comprehension. The short code is not the meaning \- it is the seed. 

As Ulam would later explain in *Science, Computers, and People*, elaborating on von Neumann’s *The Computer & The Brain*, “meaning” is the probabilistic unfolding of a short code back into complete code. Unfortunately, the Son and Holy Ghost of Team Reality were already dead when those books were published.

**Is it a good idea to build a new consciousness without first having an theory of consciousness?**

> "Philosophers are needed today more than ever to tell the AI engineers some unpleasant truths. *Gian-Carlo Rota, in "Mathematics, Philosophy and Artificial Intelligence: A Dialogue with Gian-Carlo Rota and David Sharp,"* Los Alamos Science, *Spring/Summer 1985\.*

Forty years on, the engineers still haven't hired the philosopher to break the barrier of meaning.

With or without *understanding* \- in the sense Ulam, Feynman, or Rota would define it \- we are running towards the Singularity. Despite the best efforts of the Realists, the Morons are ploughing ahead building AI without knowing how it works or having a theory of consciousness. But would building AGI without humans understanding how it works be a recipe for a societal Darwin Award? Apparently the Morons don’t care because it’s the best known recipe for blockbuster movies\! 

Looking back now I see Rota was right \- I said I’m working on AI, and the Morons think I’m crazy.
