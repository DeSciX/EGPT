# Team Reality vs. Team Oxymorons — verified quotation sheet

Every quotation below was checked against a primary text or a named reporter.
Labels: **PRIMARY** = original read · **REPORTED** = reliable witness, not the speaker's own text ·
**APOCRYPHAL** = do not use.

## ROUND 1 — Newton vs. Leibniz (1665–1716): process vs. completed object

**Newton — infinity is a limit approached, never reached.** PRIMARY
> "For those ultimate ratios with which quantities vanish are not truly the ratios of ultimate
> quantities, but limits towards which the ratios of quantities decreasing without limit do always
> converge; and to which they approach nearer than by any given difference, but never go beyond, nor
> in effect attain to, till the quantities are diminished in infinitum."

> "Therefore if hereafter I should happen to consider quantities as made up of particles... I would
> not be understood to mean indivisibles, but evanescent divisible quantities: not the sums and
> ratios of determinate parts, but always the limits of sums and ratios."

*Principia Mathematica*, Book I, Section I, Scholium following Lemma XI (1687). Motte trans. (1729),
as reprinted 1846; = Cajori ed. p. 39.

**Newton — I did not need infinitely small figures.** PRIMARY
> "I don't here consider Mathematical Quantities as composed of Parts extreamly small, but as
> generated by a continual motion."

> "In Finite Quantities so to frame a Calculus, and thus to investigate the Prime and Ultimate
> Ratio's of Nascent or Evanescent Finite Quantities, is agreeable to the Geometry of the Ancients;
> and I was willing to shew, that in the Method of Fluxions there's no need of introducing Figures
> infinitely small into Geometry."

*Introduction to the Quadrature of Curves* (1704), trans. John Harris, *Lexicon Technicum* vol. II (1710).

**The rivalry — Newton anonymously judging his own case.** PRIMARY
The Royal Society's 1712 committee "verdict":
> "For which Reasons, we reckon Mr. Newton the first Inventor; and are of Opinion, that Mr. Keill, in
> asserting the same, has been no ways injurious to Mr. Leibnitz."

*Commercium Epistolicum*, committee report, 24 April 1712, pp. 243–244.

Newton then reviewed the book anonymously in the Society's own journal — and wrote this:
> "But no Man is a Witness in his own Cause. A Judge would be very unjust, and act contrary to the
> Laws of all Nations, who should admit any Man to be a Witness in his own Cause."

"An Account of the Book entituled *Commercium Epistolicum*," *Philosophical Transactions* No. 342
(1715), published unsigned; Newton's authorship established from CUL MS Add. 3968.

**Berkeley on the whole business.** PRIMARY
> "And what are these Fluxions? The Velocities of evanescent Increments? And what are these same
> evanescent Increments? They are neither finite Quantities nor Quantities infinitely small, nor yet
> nothing. May we not call them the Ghosts of departed Quantities?"

*The Analyst* (1734), §XXXV. Note: it is a question, and its target is **Newton**, not Leibniz.

## ROUND 0 — Galileo already answered the "different sizes of infinity" claim (1638)

PRIMARY. Crew & de Salvio trans., pp. 31–33.
> "This is one of the difficulties which arise when we attempt, with our finite minds, to discuss the
> infinite, assigning to it those properties which we give to the finite and limited; but this I think
> is wrong, for we cannot speak of infinite quantities as being the one greater or less than or equal
> to another."

> "...neither is the number of squares less than the totality of all numbers, nor the latter greater
> than the former; and finally the attributes 'equal,' 'greater,' and 'less,' are not applicable to
> infinite, but only to finite, quantities."

> "But let us remember that we are dealing with infinities and indivisibles, both of which transcend
> our finite understanding, the former on account of their magnitude, the latter because of their
> smallness." (p. 26)

Galileo constructed the exact bijection Cantor later used — and drew the opposite conclusion: the
comparison itself is meaningless, not a discovery about sizes.

## ROUND 2 — Aristotle, Gauss, Kronecker vs. Cantor, Hilbert

**Aristotle — infinity is potential, never actual.** PRIMARY. *Physics* III.6.
> "The alternative then remains that the infinite has a potential existence... There will not be an
> actual infinite." (206a18–21)

> "The infinite turns out to be the contrary of what it is said to be. It is not what has nothing
> outside it that is infinite, but what always has something outside it." (206b33–207a2)

Hardie & Gaye trans. (Oxford, 1930). Greek: "λείπεται οὖν δυνάμει εἶναι τὸ ἄπειρον."

**Gauss — a completed infinity is never permitted.** PRIMARY (German).
> "So protestire ich zuvörderst gegen den Gebrauch einer unendlichen Grösse als einer Vollendeten,
> welcher in der Mathematik niemals erlaubt ist. Das Unendliche ist nur eine Façon de parler, indem
> man eigentlich von Grenzen spricht..."

> "I protest above all against the use of an infinite quantity as a completed one, which in
> mathematics is never allowed. The infinite is only a façon de parler, in which one properly speaks
> of limits."

Gauss to H. C. Schumacher, 12 July 1831; *Briefwechsel* II, p. 269. Trans. Dauben.
NOTE: the common English "as an actual entity" is a mistranslation of *als einer Vollendeten* —
Gauss's word is **completed**, which is the stronger and more useful word for this argument.

**Kronecker — the integers are given; the rest is manufacture.** REPORTED.
> "Die ganzen Zahlen hat der liebe Gott gemacht, alles andere ist Menschenwerk."
> "God made the integers, all else is the work of man."

Oral remark, Berlin Naturforscher-Versammlung 1886, as reported by Heinrich Weber,
*Jahresbericht der DMV* 2 (1893), p. 19. No Kronecker manuscript exists.

**Kronecker on Cantor personally.** REPORTED, single source.
"scientific charlatan," "renegade," "corrupter of youth" — reported by Arthur Schoenflies,
"Die Krisis in Cantor's mathematischem Schaffen," *Acta Mathematica* 50 (1927), p. 2, relayed by
Dauben. NOT from E. T. Bell — verified absent from *Men of Mathematics* by full-text search.
"Verderber der Jugend" is the best attested of the three.

**Cantor's answer.** PRIMARY.
> "denn das Wesen der Mathematik liegt gerade in ihrer Freiheit."
> "for the essence of mathematics lies precisely in its freedom."

*Grundlagen einer allgemeinen Mannigfaltigkeitslehre* (1883), §8; *Gesammelte Abhandlungen* p. 182.
On the same pages Cantor names Kummer, Kronecker and Dedekind — he knew exactly whom he was answering.

**Hilbert plants the flag.** PRIMARY.
> "Aus dem Paradies, das Cantor uns geschaffen, soll uns niemand vertreiben können."
> "No one shall be able to drive us out of the paradise that Cantor has created for us."

"Über das Unendliche," *Mathematische Annalen* 95 (1926), p. 170. (The familiar English "No one shall
expel us" is E. T. Bell's phrasing, not a literal translation.)

**Poincaré — there is no actual infinity.** PRIMARY.
> "Il n'y a pas d'infini actuel; les Cantoriens l'ont oublié, et ils sont tombés dans la
> contradiction."
> "There is no actual infinity; the Cantorians forgot this, and they fell into contradiction."

*Science et méthode* (1908), p. 212.

> "Il n'y a pas d'infini actuel, et quand nous parlons d'une collection infinie, nous voulons dire une
> collection à laquelle on peut sans cesse ajouter de nouveaux éléments (semblable à une liste de
> souscription qui ne serait jamais close dans l'attente de nouveaux souscripteurs)."
> "There is no actual infinity, and when we speak of an infinite collection we mean a collection to
> which new elements can ceaselessly be added — like a subscription list never closed, in expectation
> of new subscribers."

*Dernières Pensées* (1913), pp. 104–105. **This is the cleanest statement anywhere of infinity as a
process.**

And on the Cantorians as Platonists — Poincaré makes your two-camps point himself:
> "…qu'ils l'avouent ou qu'ils le nient, il faut donc qu'ils croient à l'infini actuel. On reconnaît
> là la théorie des idées de Platon…"
> "…whether they admit it or deny it, they must therefore believe in the actual infinite. One
> recognizes there Plato's theory of ideas…"

*Dernières Pensées*, pp. 159–160.

## ROUND 3 — Brouwer vs. Hilbert: the Frog-and-Mouse War (1920s)

**Brouwer — mathematics is not language, and the continuum is not a set of points.** PRIMARY.
> "Completely separating mathematics from mathematical language and hence from the phenomena of
> language described by theoretical logic, recognising that intuitionistic mathematics is an
> essentially languageless activity of the mind having its origin in the perception of a move of time."

*Brouwer's Cambridge Lectures on Intuitionism* (1951), ed. van Dalen (1981), pp. 4–5.

> "…the intuition of the linear continuum, i.e., of the 'between,' which is not exhaustible by the
> interposition of new units and which therefore can never be thought of as a mere collection of units."

"Intuitionism and Formalism," *Bull. AMS* 20 (1913), p. 86.

> "The belief in the universal validity of the principle of the excluded third in mathematics is
> considered by the intuitionists as a phenomenon of the history of civilization of the same kind as
> the former belief in the rationality of π, or in the rotation of the firmament about the earth."

*Cambridge Lectures*; also "Consciousness, Philosophy, and Mathematics" (1948).

**Hilbert's reply — you are taking away my fists.** PRIMARY (van Heijenoort trans.).
> "Taking the principle of excluded middle from the mathematician would be the same as, say,
> proscribing the telescope to the astronomer or to the boxer the use of his fists. To prohibit
> existence statements and the principle of excluded middle is tantamount to relinquishing the
> science of mathematics altogether."

"Die Grundlagen der Mathematik" (Hamburg lecture, July 1927), *Abh. Math. Sem. Hamburg* 6 (1928),
p. 80; trans. Bauer-Mengelberg in van Heijenoort, *From Frege to Gödel*, p. 476.
NOTE: this is **not** in "Über das Unendliche" (1926) — verified absent by OCR of the full article.

**Weyl defects to Brouwer.** PRIMARY.
> "Denn diese Ordnung ist nicht haltbar in sich, wie ich mich jetzt überzeugt habe, und Brouwer —
> das ist die Revolution!"
> "For this order is not tenable in itself, as I have now become convinced, and Brouwer — that is
> the revolution!"

"Über die neue Grundlagenkrise der Mathematik," *Mathematische Zeitschrift* 10 (1921), p. 56.

And Weyl's phrase for the continuum, which is your thesis in three German words:
> "…hier ersteht uns ein 'Kontinuum', in welches wohl die einzelnen reellen Zahlen hineinfallen, das
> sich aber selbst keineswegs in eine Menge fertig seiender reeller Zahlen auflöst; vielmehr ein
> **Medium freien Werdens**."
> "…here there arises for us a 'continuum' into which the individual real numbers do indeed fall, but
> which in no way dissolves into a set of finished-existing real numbers; rather, **a medium of free
> becoming**."

Same paper, p. 50 (section heading, p. 49). NOT in *Das Kontinuum* (1918) — that is a common
misattribution.

**Hilbert expels Brouwer; Einstein names the war and refuses to sign.**

Hilbert to Einstein, 15 October 1928:
> "…mein Entschluss, fernerhin unter keinen Umständen mit Brouwer zugleich die
> Redaktionsmitgliedschaft innezuhaben, fest und unabänderlich ist."
> "…my decision — to belong under no circumstances to the same board of editors as Brouwer — is firm
> and unalterable."

Einstein to Hilbert, 19 October 1928 — declining:
> "I consider him, with all due respect for his mind, a psychopath… I would say: 'Sire, give him the
> liberty of a jester!' … I myself cannot, for the reasons stated, sign such a letter."

Einstein to Born, 27 November 1928 — the name that stuck:
> "Wenn Hilberts Krankheit der Angelegenheit nicht einen tragischen Zug gäbe, so würde dieser
> Tintenkrieg für mich eines der drolligsten und wohlgelungensten Possenspiele sein... ich denke
> nicht daran, mit einer weiteren papieren Lanze in diesen **Froschmäusekrieg** als Kämpe
> hineinzuspringen."
> "If Hilbert's illness did not lend a tragic feature, this ink war would for me be one of the most
> funny and successful farces performed by people who take themselves deadly seriously... I do not
> intend to plunge as a champion into this **frog-mice battle** with another paper lance."

Einstein Archives 13-139 / 13-141 / 13-159; English in Dirk van Dalen, "The War of the Frogs and the
Mice, or the Crisis of the *Mathematische Annalen*," *Mathematical Intelligencer* 12:4 (1990), pp. 20, 26.
Einstein even had the file labelled DER FROSCH-MAEUSEKRIEG.
Hilbert's letter had no legal force; the board was dissolved and reconstituted without Brouwer at
vol. 101 (1929). Einstein left the board too.

## ROUND 4 — Einstein and Schrödinger vs. Bohr

**Einstein.** PRIMARY (Irene Born trans., *The Born–Einstein Letters*, letter 52, p. 91, 4 Dec 1926):
> "Quantum mechanics is certainly imposing. But an inner voice tells me that it is not yet the real
> thing. The theory says a lot, but does not really bring us any closer to the secret of the 'old
> one'. I, at any rate, am convinced that He is not playing at dice."

German: "Jedenfalls bin ich überzeugt, daß der nicht würfelt."
NOTE: Einstein wrote "the old one" (*der Alte*), not "God." The famous form is a popular rewrite.

Letter 81, 7 September 1944, p. 149 — the two camps, in his own words:
> "We have become Antipodean in our scientific expectations. You believe in the God who plays dice,
> and I in complete law and order in a world which objectively exists... No doubt the day will come
> when we will see whose instinctive attitude was the correct one."

Letter 84, 3 March 1947, p. 158:
> "…I am quite convinced that someone will eventually come up with a theory whose objects, connected
> by laws, are not probabilities but considered facts, as used to be taken for granted until quite
> recently."

*Autobiographical Notes* (Schilpp, 1949), p. 81 — the definition of the whole enterprise:
> "Physics is an attempt conceptually to grasp reality as it is thought independently of its being
> observed. In this sense one speaks of 'physical reality.'"

*Out of My Later Years*, "The Fundaments of Theoretical Physics" (1940):
> "Some physicists, among them myself, can not believe that we must abandon, actually and forever,
> the idea of direct representation of physical reality in space and time; or that we must accept the
> view that events in nature are analogous to a game of chance."

**Bohr's side.** REPORTED, and contested:
> "There is no quantum world. There is only an abstract quantum physical description. It is wrong to
> think that the task of physics is to find out how nature *is*. Physics concerns what we can *say*
> about nature."

Aage Petersen, "The Philosophy of Niels Bohr," *Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists* 19:7 (Sept 1963).
Appears nowhere in Bohr's published writings; Weisskopf publicly denied Bohr said it.

**Schrödinger, in print, 1952.** PRIMARY. "Are There Quantum Jumps? Part I," *BJPS* III:10.
> "I confess to the heretical view that their modern counterpart in physical theory are the quantum
> jumps. Or rather these correspond to the circles which the sun, the moon and the stars were thought
> to describe around the earth in 24 hours after earlier and better knowledge had been condemned."
> (p. 112)

> "This interpretation is obsolete. There is nothing to recommend it, and it bars the understanding
> of what is actually going on." (p. 115)

And REPORTED (Heisenberg's 1969 recollection of Copenhagen, Sept. 1926):
> "If all this damned quantum jumping were really here to stay, I should be sorry I ever got involved
> with quantum theory."

*Physics and Beyond*, trans. Pomerans (1971), ch. 6.

## ROUND 5 — Boltzmann alone against the phenomenologists

PRIMARY. Foreword to Part II of *Lectures on Gas Theory*, Volosca, August 1898; Brush trans., **p. 192**
(commonly miscited as p. 215):
> "In my opinion it would be a great tragedy for science if the theory of gases were temporarily
> thrown into oblivion because of a momentary hostile attitude toward it, as was for example the wave
> theory because of Newton's authority.
>
> I am conscious of being only an individual struggling weakly against the stream of time. But it
> still remains in my power to contribute in such a way that, when the theory of gases is again
> revived, not too much will have to be rediscovered."

His own footnotes name the opponents: Mach, Ostwald, Duhem, Stallo, Pearson, Poincaré.
He hanged himself in 1906. Perrin confirmed atoms in 1908.

## ROUND 6 — the family's own verdict on Gödel

PRIMARY (corpus). Ulam to Rota, taped, Gainesville, January 1974:
> "Johnny was waiting for me at the pier. His first words were that Gödel had shown that the continuum
> hypothesis was undecidable... So I said to him, 'Oh! That is because he defines what is meant by a
> set.'"

> "I asked him whether Gödel was not a little afraid that his result was nothing but a sort of super
> paradox of the existing set theory, merely a diagonal method. In a sense it is a diagonalization. He
> agreed that this was probably right..."

# DO NOT USE — verified apocryphal or misattributed

| Quotation | Verdict |

| Newton "broke Leibniz's heart" | **NOT FOUND.** Traces only to Hawking, *A Brief History of Time* (1988), uncited. Absent from Conduitt, Derham, and the Newton Project corpus. Use the anonymous self-review instead — it is documented and worse. |
| Poincaré: "Later generations will regard Mengenlehre as a disease from which one has recovered" | **APOCRYPHAL.** A hardening of "un beau cas pathologique," *Science et méthode* p. 41 — where Poincaré also credits Cantor. Use "Il n'y a pas d'infini actuel" instead; it is better and it is real. |
| Bohr: "Einstein, stop telling God what to do" | **APOCRYPHAL.** Earliest form is Bronowski (1973), who disowns it in the next sentence. Three incompatible wordings, no witness. |
| Einstein: "I still believe in the possibility of a model of reality…" | **NO LOCUS FOUND.** Use Born letter 81 or 84 instead. |
| Mach: "Have you seen one?" (of atoms) | **APOCRYPHAL.** German press reports it in hearsay grammar; SEP's Boltzmann entry does not carry it. |
| Hilbert's telescope/boxer line cited to "Über das Unendliche" (1926) | **WRONG SOURCE.** It is the 1927 Hamburg lecture, printed 1928. |
| Weyl's "Medium freien Werdens" cited to *Das Kontinuum* (1918) | **WRONG SOURCE.** It is the 1921 *Math. Zeitschrift* paper. |
| "God does not play dice" as Einstein's wording | He wrote "the old one," not "God." |

# THREE FACTS A HOSTILE READER WILL BRING

1. **Leibniz called infinitesimals fictions himself.** "je ne suis pas trop persuadé moy même, qu'il
   faut considerer nos infinis et infiniment petits autrement que comme des choses ideales ou comme
   des fictions bien fondées" (to Varignon, 20 June 1702); "ce n'étoient que des fictions, mais de
   fictions utiles" (to Dagincourt, 11 Sept 1716). The completed-object position belongs to his
   followers — Johann Bernoulli, l'Hôpital, Varignon — more than to him.
2. **Berkeley's "ghosts" is aimed at Newton,** not at Leibniz. §XXXIV cites Newton's *Quadratura* by name.
3. **Euclid's own phrasing of infinitude is potentialist** — *Elements* IX.20 says prime numbers are
   "more than any assigned multitude," which is Aristotle's potential infinity, not a completed set.
   Euclid sits awkwardly on the Oxymoron roster on the infinity question specifically; the Platonist
   charge against him rests on the axiomatic-idealization method, not on actual infinity.
