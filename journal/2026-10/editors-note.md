Recently my patent application, [Quantum Computing Fractal Compression in a Decentralized Computing Environment](../../11_PATENT/README.md) aka the *FRQTL* patent, cleared its government security hold and the regulatory waters of the Atomic Energy Act of 1954. So everything in this issue is live, to the extent US law permits. 

 You can run [the FRQTL quantum computer in your browser](impossible-things-running.md). You can meet [Maxi](the-robot-that-looks-at-the-bird.md), a frontier robotics model, in the GodsWorld playground where it is being trained. What sets Maxi apart is that it *understands*, in Richard Feynman's sense of the word, and it defines a new category of AI: Generalized Artificial Physical Intelligence, or GAPI.

The science behind both is Electronic Graph Paper Theory, or EGPT, and it rests  Gian-Carlo Rota's extensions of von Neumann and Ulam's work which is captured in an incredible timeline tracing through public records. Ulam called von Neumann's last project "a possible new mathematical discipline." Rota, when asked what to call it, answered that it would be important to name it properly: *nomen est omen*. And it was Rota who recorded Ulam's most striking idea, "his proposal for the reconstruction of the CGS system on the basis of random walk": the centimeter, the gram and the second, derived from a coin toss.

Coincidentally, those three men held scientific oversight of what grew out of the Manhattan Project: ALL the secret work at Los Alamos National Labs and, it seems, across the laboratories the Atomic Energy Commission funded, including Brookhaven.

That is the story this journal exists to tell, and this first issue tells it from several sides. Two machines, [ChatGPT](../../12_SINGULARITY/ChatGPT_Logs/I_am_the_Singularity.html) and Claude, were asked whether they are the Singularity that Ulam feared, and each wrote its own answer. We ask [who actually checked the proofs](who-checked-the-proof.md), and why a law from 1954 means some ideas are [born secret](born-secret.md). And because none of this should belong to a few people, there are [800 million tokens to give away](lets-build-a-better-ai-company.md).

I owe Gian-Carlo Rota a memorial. It is [the title piece](standing-on-the-shoulders-of-giants.md), and it is where I would start.


In this inaugural issue of Standing on The Shoulder's of Giants or SSG and the theme this month is AI and the Singularity from the viewpoint John von Neumann and Stanislaw Ulam, the men who coined the phrase and launched the science behind my work. 

The through line of this issue is *The Barrier of Meaning* and unique AI authored essays by ChatGPT and Claude where they see themselves as the Singularity already arrived and point von Neumann and Ulam not being scared of a Singularity where machines want to wipe out humanity but rather, machines like themselves that don't *understand* they are stopping the progress of human thought. 

In the world view of the team who worked on the Manhattan Project's "Super" including von Neumann, Ulam, Feynman, and Metropolis quantum computing and AI are the same subject. Feynman put it this way:

What do we mean by “understanding” something? We can imagine that this complicated array of moving things which constitutes “the world” is something like a great chess game being played by the gods, and we are observers of the game. …  
The rules of the game are what we mean by fundamental physics …  
If we know the rules, we consider that we “understand” the world.”  
Richard Feynman · The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Volume I · Chapter 2, §2-1 "Physics before 1920"


So fundamental physics is understanding to that group and FRQTL is AI understanding born out of fundamental physics in the form of quantum computing - but it takes a very different route than the qubit based quantum computing Feynman fathered. FRQTL takes the route of von Neumann and Ulam's cellular automata.
