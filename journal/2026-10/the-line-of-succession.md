Gian-Carlo Rota never wrote that he met John von Neumann. But from 1951 on he was in the rooms where von Neumann spent his time, taught by the people von Neumann worked with, and paid from the budgets von Neumann helped to set.

He spent hours with Alonzo Church at Fine Hall while von Neumann lectured there on machines and organisms. He took his doctorate at Yale in the year Yale invited von Neumann to speak on the computer and the brain. His first job was at Courant's institute, beside the Atomic Energy Commission's computer, in the last year of von Neumann's life. Then came Los Alamos, Ulam, Metropolis, and a title he held until he died.

No single fact below settles it. Taken together, it would be hard to construct a second career that just happened to pass through all the same places. Choose a question to follow one thread, or read every fact in order. Point at a fact to see the evidence for it.
