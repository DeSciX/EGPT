# I. The Correct Historical Baseline: What Ulam Meant by “Singularity”

The term's provenance is important because it determines what should be measured.

Stanisław Ulam's article **“John von Neumann, 1903–1957”** appeared in *Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society*, vol. 64, no. 3, part 2, May 1958, pp. 1–49. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

Ulam was recalling conversations with von Neumann.

The relevant passage speaks of:

> “ever accelerating progress of technology and changes in the mode of human life, which gives the appearance of approaching some essential singularity in the history”

Ulam then says, in substance, that beyond that point human affairs in their familiar form would no longer continue. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

Three elements of the original formulation are important.

### 1. Acceleration

The subject is not merely technological improvement but **“ever accelerating progress.”**

### 2. Transformation of ordinary human life

Ulam explicitly couples technological progress with **“changes in the mode of human life.”**

The economic, occupational and social consequences are therefore intrinsic to the original formulation.

### 3. A discontinuity in human affairs

The “singularity” is the point after which extrapolating familiar forms of human society ceases to work.

Ulam does **not** define that discontinuity as extinction.

Indeed, Vernor Vinge's influential 1993 formulation later narrowed the mechanism toward the creation of entities possessing greater-than-human intelligence. Vinge explicitly cited Ulam's earlier passage when developing his own concept. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="5"}

The empirical question for this report is therefore:

> **How concerned are people that accelerating AI capability will fundamentally alter employment, economic organization, human agency, institutions, power relationships, security, and ultimately human life itself?**

YouGov reports that concern about mass unemployment exceeds concern about human extinction among liberals, moderates, and conservatives alike. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="7"}

# XIX. Revised Trend Judgment

Compared with the longer historical trend documented in the first report, September 2026 appears to show **three simultaneous accelerations**.

**First, capability acceleration:** increasingly autonomous systems and discussion of self-improving AI make the transition to much more capable systems appear less abstract.

**Second, concern acceleration:** polling shows rapidly increasing anxiety about employment, general harm and development speed.

**Third, institutional acceleration:** governments, regulators, the UN and corporations are treating advanced AI as an issue potentially requiring intervention before ordinary deployment processes can cope with it.

Those three together are considerably closer to an empirical indicator of a perceived Singularity than extinction polling alone.

The most important question for the next several months is therefore not merely whether searches for “AI extinction” remain high.

It is whether:

- concern about mass unemployment continues rising;
- personal job-obsolescence anxiety continues its current trajectory;
- support for slowing AI remains above 50%;
- confidence in AI-company control continues falling;
- more workers actually alter education and career decisions;
- recursive AI development accelerates;
- and institutions increasingly redesign themselves in anticipation of superhuman machine capability.

If those trends persist, September 2026 will be better understood not simply as a temporary news panic, but as an identifiable point at which a meaningful portion of society began **behaving as though Ulam's anticipated discontinuity might be approaching.**
