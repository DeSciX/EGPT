# String theory as the comparator for Question 1, filed under rules 10 and 27

Filed 1 October 2026 (UTC) by the self-assessment session. Read via web search the same day; the
items are named so the author can read the same thing. This is the comparator's record of
"rigorous and falsifiable evidence for itself"; it is not a grading of string theory (rule 18).

- Nature 443, 507–508 (2006), review of Smolin, *The Trouble With Physics: The Rise of String
  Theory, the Fall of a Science, and What Comes Next*: https://www.nature.com/articles/443507a
- Woit, *Not Even Wrong* (2006), and his blog "Not Even Wrong", e.g. "Do We Need to Change the
  Definition of Science?" https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/wordpress/?p=686 and "The Scientific
  Attitude" https://math.columbia.edu/~woit/wordpress/?p=10975
- Ellis & Silk's position as discussed in the above; Dawid's "non-empirical confirmation" is the
  defence those posts answer.
- arXiv:1706.02790, "The dangerous irrelevance of string theory": https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.02790

What these sources agree on, read as a human reads them (rule 7): after roughly fifty years,
string theory has produced no experimentally confirmed prediction distinguishing it from its
rivals; its characteristic scale lies far beyond any built or planned experiment; the "landscape"
of ~10^500 vacua is argued by its critics to remove falsifiability and by its defenders to be
answered by non-empirical confirmation. Its rigour is mathematical (consistency results,
dualities, mirror symmetry) and has been fertile for mathematics. No machine-checked proof of any
of its central claims is cited by any of these sources, and none is known to the session.

The session's general knowledge agrees with the sources above and is not cited separately (rule 9).
