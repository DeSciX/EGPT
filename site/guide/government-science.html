The record on Rota's standing

Rota's own curriculum vitae of 1996, as the MIT mathematics server carried it and the Internet Archive captured it on 19 February 1999 (../04_ROTA/Rota_CV_1996_archived_1999-02-19.txt):

Top Secret Clearance (Air Force) 1969-1971. Q - Clearance (DOE), 1966-. SI - Clearance (NSA), 1981-.

Director's Office Fellow, Los Alamos National Laboratory, 1971-.

Medal for Distinguished Service, National Security Agency, 1992.

Kenneth Baclawski, co-author of the probability manuscript, on his own vitae (../04_ROTA/Baclawski_Kenneth_CV.txt):

7/1979 to 6/1986: Consultant to the Director. Los Alamos National Laboratory

Ulam to Rota, January 1974, in the interview about von Neumann:

"On everything. He was one of the originators, one of the "influencers" of implosion. By the way, you are my most eminent "influencee"; it is a relationship different from teacher-student."

Rota on what a Los Alamos consultant was for, at Ulam's memorial, May 1985 (../04_ROTA/The_Barrier_of_Meaning_Rota.md):

"In bygone days, the Sybil's dark mumblings would be interpreted by trained specialists, Hermeneuts they were called, who were charged with the task of expressing her cryptic messages in lapidary Greek sentences. In Ulam's case, the Los Alamos Laboratory would instead hire consultants, charged with the task of expressing his cryptic messages in the dilapidated jargon of modern mathematics."

What the machine's own reports say about the role

The two research reports GPT-5.6 Sol compiled at the author's direction, held in ../12_SINGULARITY/ChatGPT_Logs/ and ../00_OUTPUTS/, in their words. From the chronology:

"From 1966 forward Rota's career crossed academia, mathematical publishing, and the federal research establishment. He became a Los Alamos consultant in 1966 and a RAND consultant the same year; he was a Brookhaven National Laboratory consultant from 1969 to 1973; and he became a Los Alamos Director's Office Fellow in 1971."

From the report on the Director's Office Fellow title, executive summary and §2.1:

"In the closely related historical Director's Office Fellow category, Michael Waterman describes a role with exceptional institutional latitude: "Director's Fellows could come whenever they chose and could stay as long as they wished." Rota's own account captures its advisory character: he could "snoop around" the Laboratory and periodically "pop into the Director's office and have a chat with him.""

"The role involved unusual freedom of access and direct contact with senior leadership."

"This evidence supports three functional characteristics: (1) unusually flexible access to the Laboratory; (2) freedom to range across scientific subjects and organizational boundaries; and (3) proximity to senior institutional decision-making."

"This wider AEC-laboratory footprint strengthens the interpretation of Rota as a scientist operating across the federal research establishment rather than merely as an occasional Los Alamos visitor."

Two absences the same report wrote, kept as specimens

The report was asked for evidence. Twice it wrote what the evidence did not show, which is not evidence, and both sentences are kept here as specimens of the disposition the collection exists to document. At §2.1, directly after the three characteristics above:

"It does not, by itself, establish formal executive authority, a fixed reporting chain, security authorities, compensation structure, or equivalence to a federal civil-service grade."

At §5.2, on the CV that lists the Q and SI clearances:

"The present research has not yet recovered an independently accessible personnel record or surviving CV text that explicitly prints "Q clearance," so the report treats the exact clearance level as a point requiring documentary confirmation rather than inferring it solely from his classified work."

The CV was retrieved from the Internet Archive on 22 September 2026 and is quoted at the top of this page; the report's addendum records the retrieval. The absence lasted as long as it took to look, and the sentence it was written in stayed in the report.
